How This Calculator Works

Most online paycheck calculators use a flat percentage — something like federal_tax = gross × 12%. That's inaccurate. Someone earning $35,000 and someone earning $90,000 do not pay the same federal tax rate. This calculator uses the actual payroll calculation waterfall:

Gross Wages (salary + OT + commission)
↓ minus Pre-Tax Deductions (401k, HSA, health ins.)
= Federal Taxable Wages
↓ apply IRS 2026 brackets by filing status
= Federal Income Tax Withheld
↓ apply Ohio progressive brackets
= Ohio State Tax Withheld
↓ apply municipal rate (if applicable)
= Local Tax Withheld
↓ FICA: 6.2% SS (wage base capped) + 1.45% Medicare
↓ Bonus tax at 22% flat federal (supplemental wage method)
↓ minus Post-Tax Deductions
= Net Take-Home Pay

Filing Status Explained

Your federal filing status is the single biggest variable in federal income tax withholding. Choosing the wrong status on your W-4 can mean significant under- or over-withholding throughout the year.

Filing Status	Who Qualifies	Tax Impact vs. Single
Single	Unmarried, or married but filing separately from spouse	Standard baseline
Married Filing Jointly	Legally married couples combining income on one return	Lower withholding — wider brackets
Married Filing Separately	Married couples who file separate returns	Similar to Single — may increase taxes
Head of Household	Unmarried, paid >50% of home costs, qualifying dependent	Lower withholding than Single
Qualifying Surviving Spouse	Widow/widower with dependent child, within 2 years of spouse's death	Same brackets as Married Filing Jointly
Ohio Municipal Income Tax Rates

Ohio is one of the few states with a broad municipal income tax system. More than 600 cities and villages charge local income taxes on wages earned or paid within their borders. If you live in one city and work in another, you may owe tax to both — though most cities offer partial credits.
