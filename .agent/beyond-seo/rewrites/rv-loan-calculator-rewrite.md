    <p>
      Financing a brand-new camper from a dealership differs fundamentally from buying a pre-owned rig from a private party or independent lot:
    </p>

    <h3>1. Steep Depreciation & Loan-to-Value (LTV) Ratios</h3>
    <p>
      New RVs can lose 20% to 30% of their initial sticker valuation within the first two years of road service. Lenders counter this rapid depreciation by enforcing strict LTV thresholds (often 90% to 100% of dealer invoice). A 15% to 20% down payment protects the buyer from being "upside-down" or underwater on the loan.
    </p>

    <h3>2. Loan Maturity Restrictions by Age</h3>
    <p>
      While new Class A or 5th-wheel units easily qualify for 15- to 20-year amortization, lenders rarely approve 15-year financing on rigs older than 8 to 10 model years. Older used campers are generally restricted to 5- to 10-year repayment terms to guarantee the vehicle retains residual collateral value throughout the note.
    </p>

    <h3>3. Mechanical Inspections and J.D. Power / NADA Book Guidelines</h3>
    <p>
      Lenders will strictly cap your maximum financing amount to the wholesale or clean trade-in value listed in the official J.D. Power (formerly NADA) RV appraisal guides. When buying private party, ensure the purchase price aligns with book value, or you will be required to cover the shortfall out-of-pocket.
    </p>
