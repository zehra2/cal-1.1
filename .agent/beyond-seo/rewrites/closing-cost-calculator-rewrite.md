    <h2>What Are Closing Costs and What Do They Include?</h2>
    <p>
      Closing costs represent the mandatory fees, charges, taxes, and prepaid insurance reserves paid when real estate title is officially transferred from seller to buyer. These expenses are finalized at the closing table (or settlement) and are recorded line-by-line on your standardized Closing Disclosure (CD) or ALTA settlement statement.
    </p>
    <p>
      Closing expenses are divided into four primary buckets:
    </p>

    <h3>1. Lender Charges (Origination Fees)</h3>
    <p>
      Mortgage institutions charge administrative fees for evaluating, underwriting, and creating your mortgage:
    </p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Loan Origination / Underwriting Fee:</strong> Covers file preparation, risk evaluation, and loan processing. Typically charges 0.5% to 1.0% of the loan principal ($1,500 to $3,500).</li>
      <li><strong>Discount Points:</strong> Optional fees paid upfront to buy down the mortgage interest rate. One point equals 1% of the loan amount and generally lowers your rate by 0.25%.</li>
      <li><strong>Application & Credit Report Fees:</strong> Administrative costs to initiate the application and pull tri-merge credit reports ($30 to $100).</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>2. Third-Party Administrative Services</h3>
    <p>
      Professional third parties perform required verifications before mortgage issuance:
    </p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Home Appraisal:</strong> An independent licensed appraisal required by the lender to confirm the fair market valuation of the property ($450 to $900).</li>
      <li><strong>Home Inspection:</strong> A buyer-paid evaluation of the structural, electrical, plumbing, and HVAC systems ($350 to $650).</li>
      <li><strong>Boundary Survey:</strong> Confirms legal lot boundaries, easements, and fence lines ($350 to $700).</li>
      <li><strong>Flood Certification:</strong> Assesses whether the property falls within a FEMA designated special flood hazard area ($15 to $35).</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>3. Title and Escrow Charges</h3>
    <p>
      Title companies and settlement attorneys verify clean legal ownership:
    </p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Title Search & Examination:</strong> A forensic examination of public county land records to ensure no unpaid contractor liens, tax warrants, or inheritance claims exist ($200 to $450).</li>
      <li><strong>Lender's Title Insurance:</strong> Mandatory policy protecting the lender against undiscovered ownership flaws up to the mortgage balance.</li>
      <li><strong>Owner's Title Insurance:</strong> Protects the buyer's home equity for life against forged conveyances and title claims.</li>
      <li><strong>Settlement / Escrow Fee:</strong> Paid to the neutral escrow agent or closing attorney who coordinates fund disbursements and document signing ($500 to $1,200, often split 50/50).</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>4. Government Taxes & Prepaid Escrow Reserves</h3>
    <p>
      Municipal authorities and loan servicing reserves:
    </p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Transfer Taxes & Documentary Stamps:</strong> Taxes assessed by municipal, county, and state authorities to record property conveyance.</li>
      <li><strong>Recording Fees:</strong> County recorder charges to register the deed and mortgage into public record ($50 to $250).</li>
      <li><strong>Prepaid Homeowners Insurance:</strong> Lenders require the initial 12 months of hazard insurance to be paid at closing.</li>
      <li><strong>Escrow Tax Cushion:</strong> Lenders typically collect 2 to 3 months of anticipated county property taxes and insurance into an escrow cushion account.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2>How to Reduce and Negotiate Closing Costs</h2>
    <p>
      Paying closing costs does not require draining your emergency reserves. Apply these five proven strategies to minimize out-of-pocket settlement expenses:
    </p>

    <ol>
      <li>
        <strong>Shop Your Title and Settlement Services:</strong>
        Page 2, Section C of your official Loan Estimate outlines "Services You Can Shop For." You are not legally required to use your lender’s or realtor’s preferred title company. Shopping two to three independent title companies can save $500 to $1,500 on closing and administrative fees.
      </li>
      <li>
        <strong>Negotiate Seller Concessions:</strong>
        In buyers’ markets or when buying properties needing updates, buyers can request seller concessions. Conventional mortgages permit seller contributions up to 3% (under 10% down), 6% (10% to 25% down), or 9% (over 25% down). FHA loans permit up to 6%, and VA loans permit up to 4%.
      </li>
      <li>
        <strong>Leverage Lender Credits (No-Closing-Cost Mortgages):</strong>
        If you are short on liquid capital, request lender credits. The lender covers upfront closing costs in exchange for a slightly higher interest rate (e.g., +0.25% to +0.375%). While this increases your monthly payment slightly, it preserves immediate liquidity.
      </li>
      <li>
        <strong>Close at the End of the Month:</strong>
        Mortgage interest is paid in arrears. Closing on the 28th requires paying only 2 to 3 days of per-diem interest upfront, compared to paying nearly a full month of prepaid interest if closing on the 2nd.
      </li>
      <li>
        <strong>Request Title Reissue Rates:</strong>
        If the home was purchased or refinanced within the prior 5 to 10 years, ask the title underwriter for a "reissue discount." This can discount your owner’s or lender’s title insurance premium by 10% to 30%.
      </li>
    </ol>
