#### 3. What Are Closing Costs and What Do They Include? (H2)

#### 6. Strategies to Slash Closing Costs (H2)
1. **Shop for Third-Party Settlement Services:** Lenders provide a "Services You Can Shop For" list on page 2 of your Loan Estimate. Title insurance and settlement fees can vary by 20%–40% between providers.
2. **Negotiate Seller Concessions:** Conventional loans allow sellers to contribute 3%–9% toward buyer closing costs (depending on down payment); FHA allows up to 6%; VA allows up to 4%.
3. **Opt for Lender Credits / No-Closing-Cost Mortgage:** Accept a slightly higher interest rate (e.g. 0.25% higher) in exchange for the lender covering thousands in upfront cash settlement charges.
4. **Schedule Your Closing Near the End of the Month:** Closes on the 28th require only 2–3 days of prepaid per-diem interest versus 28–30 days if closing on the 2nd.
5. **Request Existing Owner Title Policy Reissue Rates:** If the seller acquired or refinanced the home within the past 10 years, ask title companies for a "reissue credit" (often 10%–30% discount).

#### 7. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) Section (H2)
1. **How much are typical closing costs on a $400,000 house?**  
   *Answer:* For a buyer with a 20% down payment ($320,000 mortgage), expect between $6,400 and $16,000 (2%–5% of the loan amount). For the seller, total costs will range from $24,000 to $36,000 (6%–9%), primarily due to broker commissions and transfer fees.
2. **Who pays closing costs: buyer or seller?**  
   *Answer:* Both parties pay distinct settlement charges. Buyers pay mortgage origination fees, appraisal, lender's title insurance, and prepaid escrow reserves. Sellers pay real estate agent commissions, state/municipal transfer taxes, attorney fees, and owner's title insurance (customary in many states).
3. **Can closing costs be rolled into the mortgage?**  
   *Answer:* On a purchase mortgage, lenders generally do not allow buyers to add closing costs directly into the loan balance if it causes the loan amount to exceed the property's appraised value. However, you can use seller concessions, lender credits (trading a higher interest rate for closing credits), or roll them in when doing a refinance.
4. **What is the difference between a Loan Estimate and a Closing Disclosure?**  
   *Answer:* A Loan Estimate (LE) is provided within 3 business days of submitting your mortgage application, giving an approximation of your closing costs. The Closing Disclosure (CD) is delivered at least 3 business days before closing, detailing the exact, binding dollar amounts.
5. **What are prepaid costs vs. closing fees?**  
   *Answer:* Closing fees are one-time administrative costs charged by the lender and third parties for processing and securing the transaction (appraisal, title search, recording). Prepaid items are recurring homeownership expenses paid in advance at settlement, such as prorated homeowner's insurance premiums and property tax escrow deposits.
6. **Can I negotiate closing costs?**  
   *Answer:* Yes. You can negotiate lender fees (such as origination and application charges), shop around for the title insurer and pest inspector, negotiate for the seller to pay a percentage of your closing costs, or negotiate real estate commissions.
7. **What is owner's title insurance and do I need it?**  
   *Answer:* While lenders require lender's title insurance to protect their financial investment, owner's title insurance protects your equity from past title defects, fraudulent claims, undiscovered liens, or unrecorded easements. It is a one-time fee paid at closing that protects you for the entire duration of your ownership.
8. **Why are closing costs higher in states like New York or Florida compared to Texas?**  
   *Answer:* Transfer taxes and mortgage recording taxes vary substantially. New York imposes steep state and city transfer taxes alongside a mortgage recording tax that can exceed 2%. Florida levies documentary stamp taxes on deeds and notes. Conversely, states like Texas have no state transfer taxes, though higher property taxes impact initial escrow reserves.

#### 3. How SBA Loan Payments Are Calculated (H2)
Explain the standard amortization formula and the impact of the **SBA Guaranty Fee**:
- Loans under $150,000: 0% guaranty fee (or waived).
- Loans $150,001 to $700,000: 3.0% of the guaranteed portion.
- Loans $700,001 to $5,000,000: 3.5% up to $1M, plus 3.75% of the portion exceeding $1M.
- Emphasize that guaranty fees can be financed into the total loan balance, slightly increasing the monthly amortized payment.

#### 4. Minimum Eligibility Requirements for Borrowers (H2)
- Operate as a for-profit enterprise located in the United States.
- Demonstrated equity investment (10%+ cash down).
- Minimum personal credit score (typically 650–680+ for 7(a); 680+ for 504).
- Proof that funds are not available from other non-government lenders on reasonable terms ("credit-elsewhere requirement").
- Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) of at least 1.15x–1.25x.

#### 5. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)
1. **What is the current interest rate on an SBA loan?**  
   *Answer:* SBA interest rates depend on the WSJ Prime Rate plus the lender's spread. For SBA 7(a) loans over $50,000 with terms over 7 years, the maximum rate is Prime + 2.75% (to 3.00%). SBA 504 fixed rates are typically lower, pegged directly to 10-year and 20-year US Treasury bond yields.
2. **SBA 504 vs. 7(a): Which is cheaper?**  
   *Answer:* For purchasing commercial real estate or large equipment over $500,000, the SBA 504 loan is generally cheaper because 40% of the financing is locked into a low, below-market fixed rate through a CDC for 20 to 25 years. SBA 7(a) loans are typically variable, making them more expensive in high-interest-rate environments.
3. **Can I use an SBA loan to buy an existing business?**  
   *Answer:* Yes. The SBA 7(a) program is the standard instrument for business acquisitions and partner buyouts. Borrowers typically must provide at least 10% equity down payment and show strong historical cash flows from the target business.
4. **How long does it take to get funded for an SBA loan?**  
   *Answer:* SBA Preferred Lenders (PLP) can approve loans in 30 to 45 days. Standard processing or complex SBA 504 real estate projects can take 60 to 90 days due to appraisal, environmental reviews, and CDC underwriting.

2. **How long can you finance an RV?**  
   *Answer:* Loan terms range from 5 to 20 years. RVs priced over $50,000, such as Class A motorhomes or high-end 5th wheels, commonly qualify for 15- to 20-year terms, allowing buyers to keep monthly payments lower.
3. **How much should you put down on an RV?**  
   *Answer:* Standard industry down payments range from 10% to 20%. Putting at least 20% down prevents you from being "underwater" on the loan during the steep initial depreciation phase of the vehicle.
4. **Is RV loan interest tax-deductible?**
